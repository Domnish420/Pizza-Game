# The 3D models

Every 3D model in the first-person world is generated by these Python scripts, run inside Blender with no window.
They build each model from shapes (bevelled boxes, lathed bowls and tubs, tubes, metaball blobs for anything soft),
paint them with textures made in numpy (tiles, marble, wood, brushed steel, mosaic, brick, asphalt), and export each
one to `assets/models/` where the game loads it by key (`Art.model("fp.<key>")`, see `ART_KEYS.md`).

```
blender -b --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 --python tools/models/build.py -- all
blender -b --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 --python tools/models/build.py -- counter oven.wood customer.3
```

`--python-exit-code 1` has to come before `--python`: Blender reads its arguments in order and runs the script as
soon as it reaches `--python`, so the flag placed after it is ignored and a failed build would still exit 0.

## Draw calls: plain colours live in the vertex colours

On a phone the cost that matters is draw calls (one per mesh per material), not triangles; see "Phone budget" in
`DESIGN.md`. So after the shadows are baked, `lib.flatten()` multiplies every plain colour (a material with no
texture, no glow and no transparency) into the vertex colours and gives those faces one shared white material (and one
for painted metal). A person draws in about 12 calls instead of 36. Textured, glowing and see-through materials stay
as they were, so use a texture only where it earns its draw call. The colours are stored as sRGB, because Godot's
Compatibility renderer uses vertex colours as they are; the preview turns them back for EEVEE.

## Checking a model before calling it done

Every change is checked with a 4-view diagnostic contact sheet (front, side, top and a three-quarter perspective),
rendered with EEVEE under a virtual display, so the sheet shades a model as the game does (`PREVIEW_ENGINE=workbench`
for a quicker sheet in flat colours):

```
xvfb-run -a blender -b --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 --python tools/models/preview.py -- counter oven.wood
xvfb-run -a blender -b --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 --python tools/models/preview.py -- all
```

Each sheet goes to `/tmp/previews/<key>.png` and the last one to `/tmp/preview.png`. The FRONT view is from the side
the player sees (`FACING` in `preview.py`: the ovens face -X, the fridge, freezer and dry store -Z, the prep
station +X, everything else +Z), and see-through materials are drawn see-through, as in the game. Look at it: back faces are
culled as they are in the game, so a face pointing the wrong way shows as a hole. The script also checks every model
and fails (exit 1) on:

- **FLOAT**: a group of parts touching nothing that does not reach the ground. Parts that hang on another model's
  wall (the ticket rail, the ladle rail, the prep tablet and shelf, the clipboard, the sign...) are listed as
  MOUNTED and allowed in their own sheet only.
- **FLIP**: a closed shell with its normals pointing inwards.

`scene.van` and `scene.kitchen` put every model where the game puts it (props at their positions, pans stretched
to the rail, customers at the hatch or seated at tables) and check the whole room, so mounted parts must meet their
wall there. `all` runs every model and both scenes. Rooms and scenes are drawn as sections (the nearest wall and the
ceiling cut away) with the three-quarter view from inside.

Then open the project in Godot (or run `godot --headless --path . --import`) so it imports the new files, and look
at them in the game.

| File | What it builds |
| --- | --- |
| `lib.py` | The toolkit: shapes, materials, generated textures, metaballs, decimation, export |
| `common.py` | The registry of keys and the materials many models share (steel, chrome, marble, oak...) |
| `rooms.py` | The van, the restaurant kitchen, the street, the dining room |
| `fixtures.py` | The counters, the pass, bin and sheeter, the four ovens, the prep station, the logs |
| `props.py` | The back of house, pans, ladles, tickets, the table, the mouse |
| `people.py` | Six customers, the delivery rider, your hands |
| `preview.py` | The diagnostic contact sheets and checks (see below) |

The look is the art style in `DESIGN.md`. The toolkit enforces most of it: boxes get rounded edges unless
a model turns `ROUND` off, materials are capped at a painted-metal look with no bump maps, the palette and shared
materials are in `common.py`, and every model gets its shadows baked in (below).

**Single-sided.** Every material is exported single-sided (`use_backface_culling`, so glTF `doubleSided` is off), as
the game and the preview both cull back faces. That is what lets an oven's chamber be an inside-out box (or, in the
dome ovens, an inside-out vault): seen from within through the mouth, invisible from outside. Inside-out on purpose
must be its own part named `chamber` (`keep` on the builder), which the FLIP check accepts.

**After the bake.** A builder can carry an `after` function, which `build.py` calls (with the builder's arguments)
once the shadows are baked: the wood oven uses it to paint soot into its vault with `lib.paint_vertices` in place of
the baked shadows (clean over the fire and across the crown, black toward the mouth).

**Painted-in shadows.** After a model is built, `build.py` bakes ambient occlusion into its vertex colours with
Cycles (`lib.bake_ao`): big faces are cut finer first so the shading has somewhere to live, a floor is put under the
model so its base darkens where it stands, and the result is tinted warm. The two rooms are baked with the counters,
oven, prep station and props standing in them (`PLACES` in `common.py`), then those are removed before export. The
game multiplies the vertex colours into the material colours. (This Blender has no denoiser, so the bake turns
denoising off; with it on, the bake fails silently and comes out black, which `bake_ao` now refuses.)

Everything is modelled directly in the game's own space (metres, Y up, the player at the origin facing -Z) and
exported without axis conversion, so a shape placed at (1.04, 1.1, 0.2) here is at (1.04, 1.1, 0.2) in the game.

Models are exported as `.gltf` + `.bin` with their textures in one shared folder, `assets/models/tex/`, so each
texture is stored once. Generated textures are cached in `tools/models/tex/` (not committed; delete it to remake
them). Budgets for a phone: a person is about 3,500 triangles, a hand about 1,900, a prop a few hundred to 3,000,
a room up to about 10,000.

This is a first pass. To replace any model with a hand-made one, drop a `.glb` with the same name in
`assets/models/`: the game loads `.glb` before `.gltf`.
